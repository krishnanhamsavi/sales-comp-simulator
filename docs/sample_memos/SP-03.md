The evidence for this program cannot support an estimate of its effect on sales. Design status is **insufficient_overlap**: only 3 treated reps found a comparable control inside the caliper, and at least 20 are needed for a credible estimate. This post-mortem therefore reports what happened and what it cost. It does not state or imply any lift, return or ROI.

**What the program did**

The APAC New-Hire Access Control Sprint (SP-03) ran from 2025-04 to 2025-06, a window of 3 months. It used a TieredAccelerator mechanic on Access Control. Eligibility was APAC reps with under 24 months' tenure (`region == 'APAC' and tenure_months < 24`), which covered 37 reps.

Participation was 59.5%, or 22 participants. The median time to first payout was 0 days, so payouts began almost immediately for the typical participant. The median payout per participant was $2,415. Payouts were concentrated: the top 20% of earners received 42.8% of payout dollars.

**What it cost**

Payout cost was $60,778 against a budget of $40,000, a budget utilisation of 151.9%. The program spent well beyond its approved budget.

**How much to trust the evidence**

Not enough to draw any conclusion about impact. Of the 37 eligible reps, 15 treated reps were analysed. Reps were excluded for three reasons:
- 8 were already being paid by another program.
- 5 had too little pre-period history.
- 5 were new hires still ramping.

The control pool held only 9 reps. Matching produced 3 matched pairs, and 12 treated reps were dropped for lack of a match.

The balance check failed. The worst remaining imbalance was in Quota attainment, with a standardised mean difference of 0.572 against a threshold of 0.10. The few matched reps are therefore not comparable on a key performance driver.

Any difference in outcomes between treated and control reps would be uninterpretable. That includes outcomes among participants. No causal estimate or confidence interval is reported.

**What to change next time**

- **Build a comparison group into the design.** Restricting eligibility to a single region and tenure band left almost no similar reps outside the program. A randomised holdout among eligible reps, or a staggered rollout, would produce a credible control group by design.
- **Avoid overlapping incentives.** Reps paid by another program had to be excluded. Coordinating program calendars, or recording overlapping payouts so they can be modelled, would keep more reps in the analysis.
- **Account for ramping and short histories.** For a new-hire population, define the pre-period and ramp exclusions before launch. This lets the team confirm in advance that enough reps will remain to measure.
- **Run a power check before launch.** Confirm that the expected number of matched pairs clears the minimum needed for an estimate.
- **Cap spend in the mechanic.** Tiered accelerators can exceed budget. A payout cap or budget-linked tier thresholds would keep costs within approval.
- **Track concentration as a design metric.** Payouts skewed toward top earners. Next time, set an expected payout distribution up front and monitor it during the program.
