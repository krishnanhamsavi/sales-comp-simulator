The evidence from this program cannot support any estimate of its effect. The measurement design is rated **unreliable**. A placebo test on 2024-07 to 2024-09, a period before the program when the true effect is zero, returned a significant effect (p = 0.006). This means the treated and comparison reps were already diverging before the bounty began. Any difference measured during the program window cannot be attributed to the bounty. No effect size, return or ROI is reported here.

**What the program did**

The Q4 Enterprise Momentum Bounty (SP-02) ran from 2024-10 to 2024-12, a window of 3 months. It paid a flat bounty on Access Control sales to reps in the Enterprise and Mid-Market segments. Of 187 eligible reps, 47 participated, a participation rate of 25.1%. Payouts were concentrated: the top 20% of participants received 37.7% of payouts. The median payout per participant was $3,300. The median time to first payout was 0 days. This suggests that many payouts may have rewarded deals already in progress when the program launched, although the data cannot confirm this.

**What it cost**

The budget was $150,000. Actual payout cost was $165,000, a budget utilisation of 110.0%. The program overspent its allocation.

**How much to trust the evidence**

Little, for causal purposes. Some parts of the design held up:

- Matching produced 30 matched pairs, and the balance check passed. The worst remaining imbalance was in target-product mix, with an SMD of 0.024 against a threshold of 0.10.
- The parallel-trends check passed, with a joint test p-value of 0.101.

These results do not outweigh the placebo failure, which directly shows a spurious "effect" where none should exist.

The analysed group is also narrow. Only 35 treated reps were analysed, and 5 of those were dropped for lack of a match. Separately, 5 reps were excluded for too little pre-period history and 10 as ramping new hires. The control pool was 137. The participation, concentration and spend figures above are reliable descriptions of what happened. They say nothing about what the program caused.

**What to change next time**

- Pay only on opportunities created after the program start date. This reduces rewards for deals already in motion.
- Set a hard payout cap so spend cannot exceed budget.
- Define the comparison group and run the placebo test before launch. If the pre-period placebo fails, adjust the design before the program starts rather than after.
- Consider randomising eligibility within segments, or staggering rollout. Segment-based eligibility makes treated and control reps systematically different.
- Collect a longer pre-period history so fewer reps are excluded and matching has more to work with.
