**What the program did**

The Q4 Access Control Attach Push (SP-01) ran from 2025-10 to 2025-12 and paid a per-unit SPIFF on Access Control to reps in the SMB and Mid-Market segments. Of 239 eligible reps, 93 earned a payout, a participation rate of 38.9%. The median participant received $1,620. The top 20% of earners took 35.9% of payouts, so spend was moderately concentrated. The median time to first payout was 0 days. That pattern is consistent with the program paying on deals that were already in motion.

On the target product, the program raised Access Control bookings by an estimated $1,466 per rep per month (95% CI $411 to $2,520), and this interval excludes zero. The effect on total bookings was $1,285 per rep per month (95% CI -$745 to $3,314), which is not statistically significant. Estimated cannibalisation of other products was 12.4% (95% CI -92.1% to 116.9%), too imprecise to interpret. Simple comparisons overstate the effect: treated minus untreated window bookings shows $20,334, and treated before-versus-after shows $3,466. Neither is a causal estimate.

**What it cost**

Payouts totalled $159,930 against a $240,000 budget, a utilisation of 66.6%. Estimated incremental bookings were $304,442 (95% CI -$176,618 to $785,501). At an assumed 70% gross margin, that is $213,109 in incremental gross profit. Net program return was $53,179 (95% CI -$283,562 to $389,921), and ROI was 33.3% (95% CI -177.3% to 243.8%). The program needed a total-bookings effect of $964 per rep per month to break even. The point estimate is above that figure, but the interval spans it, so the breakeven verdict is inconclusive.

**How much to trust the evidence**

The design checks passed. Matching produced 52 pairs with balance passing; the worst post-match SMD was 0.036, on quota attainment, against a 0.10 threshold. The parallel trends test passed (p = 0.269), and a placebo test over 2025-07 to 2025-09 passed. However, 27 treated reps had no match and were dropped. The estimate therefore describes the matched group rather than all participants. The target-product lift is credible. The total-bookings and financial-return figures are not precise enough to conclude whether the program paid for itself.

**What to change next time**

- **Plan for statistical power on total bookings.** Total bookings is the outcome that determines return, so size the eligible population and window for it, not only for target-product lift.
- **Pay only on new opportunities.** Restrict payouts to opportunities created after launch so the program does not reward deals already in the pipeline.
- **Reduce program overlap.** Limit overlap with other incentive programs so fewer reps are excluded.
- **Plan matching in advance.** Set up comparison groups before launch so fewer treated reps go unmatched.
- **Measure cannibalisation directly.** Pre-register a direct cannibalisation measure with enough data to estimate it usefully.
